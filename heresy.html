Heresy

Hell’s Tree of Knowledge

_________

PThe church’s chokehold on the world is no question, but heresy is still truly a sinister action. Those at odds with the safety of others, willing to cut corners just for another dollar are the primary inhabitants of this ring. And the ring - oddly enough - seems the simplest to survive. All one has to do is obey the rules of the rainy cathedral city, all implemented for the safety of other sinners. Some are obvious - such as no violence - others seem more counteractive - such as not turning in or apprehending rulebreakers. On the first day of the final month, survivors are turned into banshees and allowed to roam free. The issue comes from this being promised once the year’s souls are released into the ring. Then demons simply deliver the knowledge they need to know, “You will be given a second chance, this opportunity is not given to everyone,” and any further detail must be asked of them.

The more harm a sinner has caused once in hell, the less likely they’ll be given a second chance. Most are killed, bodies disposed of in the sprawling catacombs under the city. Some are given a second chance as a punishment - husks are deprived of the abilities to understand specific emotions (typically their driving emotions) and unable to die by standard means. Each apparition is only allowed to make one husk every fifteen years due to the stigma around and the unethicality of depriving a souled being of their soul. This is the only ring where a person is able to be kept alive after having their soul removed.

Banshees

Banshees are spirits in hell, unconstrained from their physical forms. When a sinner in the Heresy Ring patiently and obediently waits out their sentence, their bodies are flayed off, releasing all the nonphysical parts that make you you. A good thing to note is that banshees aren’t ghosts. Ghosts are moldable but ultimately physical constructs made to contain a soul ripped from the cycle until the one who cast the spell dies, while banshees are completely incorporeal unless they wish to be with their full death coming once their soul grows too weak to continue persisting. Banshees are the demonic counterpoint of ghosts.

Banshees are known for their horrific screams, able to cause damage just from their cries. While eardrum destruction and throwing items about the room are well known effects, there are far more effects. Flaying skin, breaking bones, dismembering, echolocation, the list goes on. A banshee’s cries are the most distressing thing you can hear. They sound as if they’re living individuals in severe agony. It’s impossible to tell what you’re hearing until your ears begin ringing.

Husks

We do not create husks. We create punishments.

There are two types of being in this world. Souled and Soulless. And never should one become the other. Beings without a soul cannot gain one - for that to happen would painfully rewrite their biology and capabilities and the soul would become impossible to remove. Likewise, souled beings should be killed if they lose their soul - losing a soul is essentially the death of one's entire identity. Husks should never be created. Thankfully, it is nigh impossible for the former to happen as the soulless are unable to enter hell unless in absurdly specific scenarios.

As for the latter… in the Heresy Ring, every fifteen years a husk is allowed to be created. One sinner allowed to have their entire self irreversibly ripped from them as a punishment for breaking the rules. It's impossible to know how much of the resulting damage comes from knowledge of what was done to them, and how much is from the soul's removal.

Apparition of Heresy, Sebastian Mordrego

Sebastian Mordrego is a name any sailor around the Rostbor Harbor remembers with regret. Those who’d attempt to sail down a shortcut drawn by the mapmakers of the area - through the rocky ridges of the Faaso Ocean - would find their boats decimated by this monstrous kraken. None of the crew ever died though, instead they found themselves returned to dry land. Their reign of terror ended once the citizens banded together and poached the massive beast, humiliating them by leaving their corpse on the sand to rot. It wasn’t long after their death that the sailors learned the Fasso Ocean was particularly deadly, with enough storms and sirens to reduce the average trading vessel to an underwater graveyard.

Enraged by the circumstances of their early demise, the ring of Heresy turned into a death trap for the cocky. Oh yes, sinners are allowed much more freedom than in other rings, almost allowed to live in their own little society, however the strict rules of their paradise - while easy to avoid breaking - are tantalizing to the sinners. A tree of knowledge, easy to avoid but once you know of it, it’s impossible to stop thinking about it. Those who serve their sentence without breaking a rule are granted a second chance under their watchful eye, however to those who fall to temptation, death is on the horizon. Set upon by armies of soulless demons ecstatic to please both a merciful and sadistic god.

Reaper of Heresy, Vladimir Mikhailov

Vladimir is the interesting sort. Most reapers are chosen as a reward - either to sooth old wounds or due to their displays of loyalty. Vladimir’s selection was a punishment. While alive, he was manipulative, always suggesting shortcuts and rebellion regardless of the consequences while he reaped the benefits. Naturally, once he was in the Heresy Ring, he immediately broke one of the rules. For it he lost his arms, but not his life. He just barely managed to escape the encounter. Hiding, running, and being hunted for all of his sentence. His survival tactic was what he’d done for his whole life, making others do the dirty work for him. He overwhelmed his pursuers to the point he could go under the radar.

Somehow, by a stroke of stupidly good luck, Vladimir managed to wait out his entire sentence despite being marked for death since the day he landed in the ring. After much debate for whether or not he should still be executed, almost impressed by how durable he was, Sebastian had some cruel mercy for him, returning his rotted arms now tied to his coat pauldrons… but his soul was ripped out and returned to the cycle. No longer is he able to feel fear or misery. He obeys the rules now, his life rooted in the same place he tried so desperately to escape. He’s alive, but no one knows just how much of Vladimir is left.

Wanderer of Heresy, Naadir Belketh

Naadir Belketh hates the living world more than anyone else down in Hell, only finding safety and comfort in death. Flesh is weak, disgusting, and needn’t exist at all. It’s near impossible to imagine this banshee having once been a man. When Naadir found out what laid for sinners should they obey the rules, not a toe was stepped out of line. The obsession with death in life had practically been rewarded… except for one issue. The heresy at the time knew why Naadir was so eager to stay in line and - as a so called, “test of loyalty,” - rewarded the obedience displayed with the removal of the soul - a punishment saved for the worst of the worst, not for those like Naadir - trapping Naadir inside of an unwanted body.

Depression and desperation struck, Naadir refusing to care for a loathed and unwanted form. Flesh rotted, burned off with fire and acid, and yet wasn’t enough. Freedom was so, so close and yet was torn away at the last second out of a petty desire for total control. Heresy was replaced, Naadir promoted to wanderer, and yet it wasn’t enough. Not until Naadir discovered a spell to replace flesh with soul. A ghost, not a full banshee, but it was close enough. Freedom was met with euphoria, the hatred of flesh only grew as that long dead body shattered on the pavement and flesh became null. It’s ill-advised to enter a graveyard for any reason but respecting the dead, lest you come face to face with the spirit who knows how to manipulate souls.
