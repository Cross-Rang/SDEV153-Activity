What is The Underworld Project?

Everyone dies one day, and their souls move on. It's just how things work. We mourn the losses and move on, carrying the weight of their lives on our backs until it catches us too. The soul cycle is an interesting one, one many scholars have peered into to the best of their abilities. Our souls - to put it simply - don't belong to us. when we die, they're tossed back into the cycle of reincarnation to be brought back into a new body. While not every living being has one, sapient beings who reproduce sexually require one to exist as the natural magic of the world is unable to inhabit them. As we live our day to day lives, we imprint on this soul, molding it into something new and unique. Think of it not as a string of unchangeable code, but a piece of clay passed down through generations. Unless cursed to the soul itself, it doesn't matter who you used to be. It's beautiful in a sense... unless you happen to be the kind of person who doesn't care about harming others. No one is inherently pure, we all are the villains in another's story, however every bad deed weighs on your soul, making it harder and harder for one to be subsumed back into the cycle of reincarnation that claims us all. The wicked never truly move on. Should your sins become too great, your name living in everyone’s heads as the person they mustn't become, you won’t be able to leave the plain of life, stuck in suspension between life and death. In order to purge the sins that cursed you to remain trapped, you must go through suffering equal to what you made others go through.

Hell.

Every continent has its own version of hell, a place where entities keep the cycle balanced and deliver the purified souls to the angels so they might just get the chance to live a better life. Demons, constructs, lost souls, and the like are all things you will witness if you find yourself here. Trapped in Limbo, sorted into the ring of which you most fit, and tormented until your soul is finally freed by victims of your own making. Your only means of escape being to repent to those you've wronged. You'll be spared the worst fate, either given a second chance to pay your dues or a mercifully quick death. If you attempt to fight back, give in to your worst traits, dig your feet in the sand and continue to insist you don't know what you did to deserve such a fate, consequences will increase. And if you were especially bad, you will meet one of the dukes of Hell face to face. It is nearly impossible to fight back against a duke, much less kill them. They will hand you your punishment on a silver platter, regardless of if they have to force it into your hands. The denizens of hell aren't bound by our morality; that's what makes them so terrifying, and so effective at purifying souls.

The Continent

As previously stated, each continent has its own version of Hell. This is mostly for ease of writing as there are certain rings where a language barrier would be worse than the punishment itself. The specific continent for this story is Weodirrus, and archipelego found in-between the Fasso and Nagayas oceans.

The continent itself is rather small, being comparable to Australia in size. In the north, where it connects to the rest of the landmass, is the Azahn Peaks, a mountain range home to many extremist kinds of creatures such as dragons, lamia (half-dragons), minotaurs, and harpies. To the west is a cliff that leads to Belahn, a seaside country bordering the siren infested Fasso Ocean. This place is only inhabited by selkies as sirens prey on pretty much every other species. Moving back east is the capital, Tusha Village - a traveler's point on a plateau overlooking all of the continent save for the Azahn Peaks. furter east is Tepointe, a forest country mostly inhabited by fae (general term for insectoid creatures), kelpies (humanoid frogs), and mages (fauns with extreme magic capacity). The river running through the continent is unnamed as every country tends to have a different term for it, however travelers know it as Siren Gully due to it having previously been home to the now extinct river siren species. South of Tepointe is Western and Eastern Tula, extremely rich counties. Eastern Tula is known more for its art - cullinary, threadwork, architecture, and galleries in general - while Western Tula is more industrial and factory driven. This is because, bordering Western Tula across the river, there is the DeadLands, formerly known as the Kingdom of Lanternbrush. The land is burnt out and cursed as nothing ever regrew here due to the plague that ravaged the kingdom a century and a half ago. The surrounding river and lake is poisoned from it, with all inhabitants avoiding it as if it's a mass grave. West of the Deadlands is the Outlands - an uninhabited bit of the country mostly used for traveling and trade. Rest points, farms, shops, and a few isolated inhabitants are all you'll find here. North of the Outlands is Lurewick, a town surrounded on all sides by forest that is home to the only solely human settlement due to the cult in town that preaches that all nonhumans are evil despite the fact that they had the town curse broken by a pair of nonhumans. This settlement is very close to imploding on itself for the third time, as both kingdoms before it fell into ruin - once from a revolt, the second due to the king dying and the princess disappearing into the night. Off the coast in the Fasso Ocean is the Major and Minor Arche, a pair of islands ruled by sphinxes known for their technological developments despite being formerly devastated by a war launched by the mainland. Off the coast of the Outlands in the Nagayas Ocean is Rostbor Harbor, a small trading island bordered by a mountainous ridge separating the two oceans known as the Mordrego Pass, named after the kraken the inhabitants murdered after failing to understand their attempts to protect them from the Fasso Ocean. Weodirrus's population is rather spread out, however the whole population could theoretically all fit on the Arche Islands

Hell always takes on the same form as its continent counterpart, with a few changes. The rule of thumb is that what the continent does affects hell, however what hell does won't affect the continent. In this place, the rings replace major settlements. Limbo - and Fraud which is found within Limbo - exist in a leveled Tepointe surrounded by a moat of ice the size of the Arche islands. Lust replaces the Dead Lands, with the bordering lake being a lake of blood. Gluttony exists around a pair of abandoned Arche Islands. Greed is found where Western Tula was, with its sister ring Treachery replacing Eastern Tula. Anger is found within an overgrown Lurewick. Heresy is located in a dilapidated Rostbor Harbor. Violence - fittingly - is found where Tusha Village once stood. Belahn, the Azahn Peaks, and the Outlands don't have a hell equivalent and are uninhabited shells of their living world selves.

Marriage

Marriage in hell is something I had to think for a while about because, due to the various shapes of the demons, rings weren't gonna cut it.

The purpose of marrige is to designate a significant bond. This bond is not always romantic. Of the four married couples in the cast, only one pair got married for romance's sake - those two being Naadir and Kudur. The bonds can be anything from a deep friendship, a rivalry, or a simple statement of "you're the reason I'm down here." It isn't a massive celebration or a legal statement, its a quiet endeavor. An exchanging of vows, an exchanging of gifts, and nothing more.

The gifts in Hell are very much not rings. In hell, it is seen that there are two ways to be dangerous - physical and magical. The traditional gift exchange embeds itself into this view. The physically stronger partner recieves a mask designed similar to a muzzle - covering the mouth and framing the eyes - while the magically stronger partner recives a pair of gauntlets that cover at least the palms of their hands, each studed with matching jewels, although some may be hidden. These gifts symbolize making oneself weaker for their partner's protection. They are typically only removed within the company of one's spouse or unless necessary for an activity.

(left) The best example of this culture is Enid and Virgil Delekarte (and definitely not because they were the only two I designed with this in mind). Their reason for marriage was ironic, Virgil wanted Enid to reject them so they'd feel like they could have at least some consequence for her fate but Enid - assuming it was an apology - accepted. Both Enid's gauntlets and Virgil's mask are cut from the same leather and studded with the same diamonds. Enid - being a mage - is the stronger of the duo magically, and Virgil - while not incapable magically - tends to rely on their strength while hunting souls. While not a romantic endeavor, their bond is built on the care and respect they have for one another... just in opposite directions.

Demon Identification Tables

Demons have a special structure of demon identification, split between the type of demon, the role they play, and how they're created.

Type
Construct This demon was built and animated with magic instead of being born.
Undead This demon is developed and born of another demon.
Ethereal This demon has connections with the Soul Plane and therefore doesn't have a solid body

Role
Working This demon is sapient.
Lax This demon is nonsapient.
Hazard This demon is an envioronmental hazard

Creation
Native This demon is native to hell.
Lost This demon is a native to the living world.
Case-by-Case This demon could come from either means.
